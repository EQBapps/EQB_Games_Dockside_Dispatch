# EQB Games: Mobile Top Charts & Genre Research

**Snapshot date:** 17 November 2026 · **Market:** United States store charts, plus worldwide market data

**Prepared for:** EQB Business Solutions, gaming division planning

> **About the data.** Apple and Google don't publish chart history, so these lists come from third-party trackers that read the store charts several times a day (AppBrain, Zing, StoreSignal, AppMarketSpy, Applyra, Similarweb). Ranks change daily and vary by country. Treat them as a snapshot of what is winning right now. Market figures come from Sensor Tower, AppMagic and GameRefinery, and they are estimates. Genre labels are our own classification. Where we don't know a newer title well, its genre is marked *(inferred)*.

---

## 1. Executive summary

1. **Simple puzzle games dominate the free charts.** About 6 or 7 of the top 10 free games on both stores are casual or hybrid-casual puzzles: sorting, traffic or bus-jam, block, ring-rotation and logic puzzles. They are cheap to build, quick to test and easy to market with ads.
2. **The remaining free slots go to platforms with huge budgets** (Roblox, Fortnite, PUBG) **and long-running casual franchises** (Township, Gossip Harbor). A new studio can't compete with these directly.
3. **The paid charts barely change and are led by indie games.** Minecraft, Geometry Dash, Stardew Valley, Balatro, Bloons TD 6 and Plague Inc. have held their places for years. Prices run from $0.99 to $9.99, and most sit at **$4.99–$6.99**. A small team can reach this chart with one excellent game.
4. **Most revenue comes from free-to-play mid-core and casual live-service games.** These include 4X strategy, MOBAs, match-3, merge and board/social-casino games. They need big marketing budgets and years of ongoing updates.
5. **Where the market is growing:** in H1 2026, hybrid-casual revenue grew about 23% (to $2.4B) and puzzle grew about 20% (to $8.1B). Overall spending was flat and downloads fell 12%. Studios now earn more from each player instead of chasing more installs.
6. **Recommendation for EQB:** start with **hybrid-casual puzzle games** (free, earning from ads and in-app purchases), built through a quick prototype-and-test pipeline. In parallel, build **one premium indie game** for the paid charts. Avoid 4X, MOBA, battle royale and gacha RPG for now.

### What the paid charts tell us
- **Almost nothing changes.** Most of the top 10 are 5–20 years old. Players pay for games they already trust from PC or console, or from word of mouth.
- **Three genres keep winning:** roguelike/roguelite (Balatro, Slay the Spire, Warhammer Survivors, Absolum), cozy or sandbox crafting (Minecraft, Stardew, Terraria), and strategy/TD/sim (Bloons, Plague Inc., Mini Metro).

- **A bundled alternative:** Apple Arcade and Google Play Pass fund premium games without ads or IAP. Google is also adding game trials and "buy once, play anywhere" pricing.

## 6. Trends to build around

1. **Sort and escape mechanics are the leading format right now.** Arrows Puzzle Escape topped worldwide downloads in May. Bus-jam, parking and colour-sort games fill the US top 10. The core mechanic is easy to copy, so the theme, game feel and meta-progression are what set a game apart.
2. **A cute character on a classic puzzle works.** Meowdoku took the #1 spot in both stores by putting a cat mascot on a familiar logic puzzle.
3. **Revenue now depends on hybrid monetisation and progression gating.** Colony Flow became the top hybrid-casual puzzle game by IAP while having *fewer* downloads than its rivals, because it gates content behind progression. The free-chart winners combine rewarded ads, ad-removal and "no ads" bundles, boosters, a battle pass and collectibles.
4. **Merge-2 mechanics are being added to other genres** (e.g. Coin Master's "Order Express", Bingo Voyage). Gossip Harbor's revenue grew 468% over the period.
5. **Seasonal live-ops matter.** Halloween events drove October revenue for the top games. Even small games need a seasonal event calendar.
6. **Earning more from each player.** Downloads fell 12% in H1 2026 while revenue per download rose about 11%. Retention and monetisation design now matter more than install volume.
7. **India, Brazil and Southeast Asia bring volume; the US, Japan and China bring revenue.** Choose ad-heavy or IAP-heavy monetisation per market.

### 8.1 Dockside Dispatch: hybrid-casual sort puzzle {: .newpage }

**Pitch:** *Run the busiest little harbour in the world. Clear the container yard, fill each ship with its colour and send it off with a blast of the horn.*

| Snapshot | |
|---|---|
| **Genre** | Hybrid-casual sort / traffic-escape puzzle |
| **Audience** | Broad casual, 18–55; the same players as bus-jam and colour-sort games |
| **Session** | 3–8 minutes (3–5 levels); one level takes 30–90 seconds |
| **Orientation** | Portrait, one thumb |
| **Proven by** | Sort/escape puzzles (Block Out!, Bus Fever, Magic Sort! in the US top 10) |
| **Our twist** | 1-slot and 2-slot containers add a packing layer; every ship departure is a rewarding set piece |

**How it plays**

- A yard of stacked shipping containers sits above the quay. Each container has a colour.
- Ships dock at 2–3 berths. Each ship wants one colour and has 3–6 slots.
- Tap a container with a clear path to the crane. It loads onto a matching ship, or waits in a 5-slot buffer area.
- A full ship sails away and the next one docks. Clear every ship to win. You lose if the buffer fills up.
- New elements arrive every 10–15 levels: locked containers (need a key), hidden-colour containers, refrigerated containers on a timer, 2-slot "long" containers and stacked cranes.

**Game loops**

<div class="loop meta"><div class="loop-label">Meta loop · per session / daily</div><div class="steps"><span>Win levels</span><b>→</b><span>Earn coins + port stars</span><b>→</b><span>Spend stars to restore harbour landmarks</span><b>→</b><span>New district unlocks</span><b>→</b><span>Daily shipping contracts <em>↺</em></span></div></div>
<div class="loop long"><div class="loop-label">Long-term loop · weekly / seasonal</div><div class="steps"><span>Seasonal "trade routes" (new themed ports)</span><b>→</b><span>Weekly leagues</span><b>→</b><span>Co-op crew events</span><b>→</b><span>Season pass rewards <em>↺</em></span></div></div>

**Monetisation**

- Capped interstitials after levels from level ~15. Rewarded ads for a "second chance" (+2 buffer slots) or double coins.
- Boosters: *Extra Berth*, *Crane Lift* (remove any container) and *Shuffle*. The first few are free so players learn them.
- No-ads pack ($4.99), starter bundle ($1.99) and a monthly *Harbour Pass* ($4.99) with cosmetic ships.

**Art & audio:** chunky, bright low-poly 3D with a warm sunset palette. Container drops make a satisfying thunk and each departing ship sounds its horn. These "juice" moments are what the ad creatives will show.

**Risks & mitigation**

- *Very crowded format.* Win on ad creatives and game feel; produce 10+ creatives before building more content.
- *Difficulty spikes cause players to leave.* Tune a sawtooth difficulty curve using level-by-level funnel data.

**Prototype test (2 weeks):** 30 levels, no meta layer. Pass bar: CPI ≤ $0.80 (US test), D1 retention ≥ 40% and day-0 playtime ≥ 15 minutes.

### 8.2 Knot Loose: rope-escape puzzle {: .newpage }

**Pitch:** *Every tangle has an order. Pull the right rope first, and watch a woven tapestry come to life.*

| Snapshot | |
|---|---|
| **Genre** | Escape / unravel puzzle |
| **Audience** | Casual, 25–55; people who like calm "brain" games and ASMR |
| **Session** | 5–10 minutes; one level takes 20–60 seconds |
| **Orientation** | Portrait, one thumb |
| **Proven by** | Escape puzzles (Arrows Puzzle Escape was #1 worldwide by downloads in May 2026) |
| **Our twist** | Ropes cross *over and under* each other, so only the top rope can move. The cleared board reveals part of a tapestry. |

**How it plays**

- The board holds ropes. Each rope ends in an arrowhead showing which way it slides.
- Tap a rope to pull it out. If another rope blocks it, it snaps back and you lose 1 of 3 hearts.
- Layering adds depth: a rope pinned *under* another can't move until the top one is gone.
- New elements: pinned knots (tap twice), ropes that drag their neighbours, colour gates and slow-burning fuse ropes (a soft timer).

**Game loops**

<div class="loop meta"><div class="loop-label">Meta loop · per session / daily</div><div class="steps"><span>Finish a level set</span><b>→</b><span>Complete a tapestry</span><b>→</b><span>Add it to your album</span><b>→</b><span>Unlock rope styles (silk, chain, neon)</span><b>→</b><span>Daily knot challenge <em>↺</em></span></div></div>
<div class="loop long"><div class="loop-label">Long-term loop · weekly / seasonal</div><div class="steps"><span>Weekly themed tapestry event</span><b>→</b><span>"Perfect pull" leaderboard (no mistakes)</span><b>→</b><span>Seasonal albums <em>↺</em></span></div></div>

**Monetisation**

- Rewarded ads for hints and to refill hearts. Interstitials with soft caps.
- Hint packs, an "Untangle" booster that removes one rope, cosmetic rope styles, and a no-ads pack ($4.99).

**Art & audio:** soft, textured 2D yarn and rope on fabric backgrounds. Calm, tactile sound (rope swish, wooden peg clicks). The brand promise is "relaxing but clever".

**Risks & mitigation**

- *Escape-puzzle clones are everywhere.* The over/under layering and tapestry reveal must come through in the first 5 seconds of every ad.
- *Running out of levels.* Build a procedural level generator plus a solver that checks each level can be won, so designers curate levels instead of hand-building them.

**Prototype test (1–2 weeks):** 40 levels plus the generator. Pass bar: CPI ≤ $0.70, D1 retention ≥ 40% and a playable-ad click-through rate in the top quartile.

### 8.3 Paw Logic: daily logic puzzles with pets {: .newpage }

**Pitch:** *Solve a picture-logic puzzle and a pixel pet comes to life, then moves into your cozy café.*

| Snapshot | |
|---|---|
| **Genre** | Classic logic puzzles (nonogram first; kakuro and star-battle later) with a mascot and collection layer |
| **Audience** | 25–60, skewing female; daily habit players with high lifetime value who are open to subscriptions |
| **Session** | 5–15 minutes, often several times a day |
| **Orientation** | Portrait; supports tablets |
| **Proven by** | Meowdoku took #1 free on both stores by putting a cat mascot on a familiar logic puzzle |
| **Our twist** | Each solved puzzle *is* a pet. Collecting pets fills a café that players decorate and visit every day. |

**How it plays**

- A standard nonogram grid (5×5 up to 20×20). Use the row and column clues to fill cells.
- The finished grid turns into an animated pixel pet that "adopts" the player.
- A friendly mascot hosts each puzzle and gives gentle mistake warnings (players can turn this off for hard mode).
- New puzzle types unlock over time to keep the game fresh without leaving the brand.

**Game loops**

<div class="loop core"><div class="loop-label">Core loop · 2–8 min per puzzle</div><div class="steps"><span>Read the clues</span><b>→</b><span>Fill / cross out cells</span><b>→</b><span>Rows complete with a sparkle</span><b>→</b><span>Pet revealed and animated</span><b>→</b><span>Coins + pet adopted <em>↺</em></span></div></div>
<div class="loop meta"><div class="loop-label">Meta loop · daily</div><div class="steps"><span>Daily puzzle + streak</span><b>→</b><span>Spend coins on café furniture</span><b>→</b><span>Pets visit and "react" to the décor</span><b>→</b><span>Complete pet families (cats, birds…) <em>↺</em></span></div></div>
<div class="loop long"><div class="loop-label">Long-term loop · monthly</div><div class="steps"><span>Monthly calendar (one pet per day)</span><b>→</b><span>Themed puzzle packs</span><b>→</b><span>Rare seasonal pets</span><b>→</b><span>Share café screenshots <em>↺</em></span></div></div>

**Monetisation**

- **Paw Pass subscription** ($4.99/month or $29.99/year): no ads, unlimited hints, every archive puzzle and exclusive pets. Offer a 7-day free trial.
- Free players see banner ads plus a capped interstitial, and use rewarded ads for hints. One-off themed packs are $1.99–$3.99.

**Art & audio:** soft pastel UI with crisp pixel-art pets and gentle lo-fi music. Accessibility is a priority: large-grid mode, colour-blind palettes and left-handed layout.

**Risks & mitigation**

- *Many nonogram apps already exist.* Compete on character, collection and a fair ad experience, not on the puzzle itself.
- *Content volume.* Use a pixel-art pipeline plus a uniqueness solver (checks every puzzle has exactly one solution) so 365+ puzzles a year are achievable.

**Prototype test (2 weeks):** 50 puzzles plus a basic café. Pass bar: D1 retention ≥ 45%, D7 ≥ 18% and a trial-start rate ≥ 3% of new users.

### 8.4 Spice Route Merge: merge-2 story adventure (Phase 2) {: .newpage }

**Pitch:** *Revive your grandmother's travelling food stall. Merge ingredients, serve hungry customers and uncover family secrets along an old trade route.*

| Snapshot | |
|---|---|
| **Genre** | Merge-2 with narrative and renovation |
| **Audience** | 30–60, skewing female; high-spending casual players |
| **Session** | 5–15 minutes, several times a day (energy-gated) |
| **Proven by** | Gossip Harbor: #3 grossing worldwide in October 2026 ($74M) and revenue up 468% over 19 months |
| **Our twist** | A food and culture theme with collectible recipe cards; each chapter is a new market town with its own ingredients |

**How it plays**

- A 7×9 board. Tap *generators* (spice basket, clay oven, tea kettle), which costs energy, to produce items.
- Merge two identical items into the next tier (e.g. seed → chilli → chilli bunch → spice jar).
- Customers at the stall order specific items. Filling orders earns coins, XP and story stars.
- Spend stars to restore the stall and town locations, which moves the story forward.

**Game loops**

<div class="loop core"><div class="loop-label">Core loop · seconds to minutes</div><div class="steps"><span>Tap a generator (−energy)</span><b>→</b><span>Merge items up the chain</span><b>→</b><span>Fill a customer order</span><b>→</b><span>Coins + XP + stars</span><b>→</b><span>New orders appear <em>↺</em></span></div></div>
<div class="loop meta"><div class="loop-label">Meta loop · daily</div><div class="steps"><span>Spend stars on renovation tasks</span><b>→</b><span>Story scenes unlock</span><b>→</b><span>New generator / ingredient chain</span><b>→</b><span>Energy refills over time <em>↺</em></span></div></div>
<div class="loop long"><div class="loop-label">Long-term loop · weekly / seasonal</div><div class="steps"><span>Festival events (separate event board)</span><b>→</b><span>Recipe card album sets</span><b>→</b><span>New chapter / town every 2–4 weeks</span><b>→</b><span>Team events <em>↺</em></span></div></div>

**Monetisation:** energy and gem bundles, premium generators, an event pass ($9.99), limited-time packs and a piggy bank. This genre makes most of its money from a small group of high spenders, so an LTV model is needed before launch.

**Art & audio:** rich, warm 2D illustration with expressive character portraits. Each region gets its own music theme.

**Risks & mitigation**

- *Expensive to acquire players and needs years of live-ops.* Only start this once a Wave 1 game is profitable and the team has live-ops experience. Consider a publishing partner.
- *Economy tuning is complex.* Hire or contract an experienced F2P economy designer.

**Gate to start:** at least one Wave 1 title is profitable after marketing costs, plus an economy design spec has been reviewed.

### 8.5 Lantern Crawl: premium roguelite {: .newpage }

**Pitch:** *The dungeon is dark and your lantern is all you have. Every step burns oil, and every lens you find changes what the light can do.*

| Snapshot | |
|---|---|
| **Genre** | Turn-based roguelite dungeon crawler |
| **Audience** | Core and mid-core players, 18–40; fans of games like Balatro and Slay the Spire |
| **Session** | One run = 12–20 minutes; can pause anywhere |
| **Price** | $5.99 up-front, no ads, no IAP (optional supporter soundtrack) |
| **Platforms** | iOS + Android first; PC (Steam) / Switch port planned |
| **Proven by** | Roguelites are the strongest premium genre: Balatro, Slay the Spire, Warhammer Survivors and Absolum are all in the paid top 10–20 |
| **Our twist** | *Light is the resource and the weapon.* Your light radius decides what you can see, what enemies can do and how far you can go. |

**How it plays**

- Portrait, one-handed, tile-based. Each move costs 1 oil.
- Enemies in darkness are hidden. Enemies inside your light radius can be targeted, and some only move when they are lit.
- Lenses change how the light works. A **red lens** freezes lit enemies. A **blue lens** reveals secret walls. A **prism** splits the light into 3 beams. A **flicker wick** gives a random burst.
- Each run is 3 floors plus a boss. Between floors, choose 1 of 3 upgrades: a lens, a relic or more oil capacity.

**Game loops**

<div class="loop core"><div class="loop-label">Core loop · per floor (3–6 min)</div><div class="steps"><span>Step (−oil)</span><b>→</b><span>Light reveals tiles / enemies</span><b>→</b><span>Fight, sneak or loot</span><b>→</b><span>Find the stairs</span><b>→</b><span>Choose 1 of 3 upgrades <em>↺</em></span></div></div>
<div class="loop meta"><div class="loop-label">Meta loop · per run</div><div class="steps"><span>Run ends (win or die)</span><b>→</b><span>Earn embers</span><b>→</b><span>Unlock new lenses, relics, lamplighters</span><b>→</b><span>Fill out the codex</span><b>→</b><span>Start a new run <em>↺</em></span></div></div>
<div class="loop long"><div class="loop-label">Long-term loop · weeks to months</div><div class="steps"><span>"Dimmer" difficulty levels (1–12)</span><b>→</b><span>Daily seeded run + leaderboard</span><b>→</b><span>Free content updates</span><b>→</b><span>Paid expansion <em>↺</em></span></div></div>

**Business model:** $5.99 at launch with a launch discount to $4.99. A free demo (first floor) helps word of mouth. Pitch the game to Apple Arcade and Google Play Pass in parallel, since a platform deal could fund development.

**Art & audio:** high-contrast pixel art in which darkness is a real design element, with glowing light cones and warm-versus-cold colour. Ambient, tense soundtrack.

**Risks & mitigation**

- *Discoverability.* Premium games depend on store featuring, press and streamers. Build a community early (Discord, devlogs, Steam wishlist page).
- *Long development time.* Lock the core "light" mechanic in a 6-week vertical slice before building more content.

**Prototype test (6 weeks):** a vertical slice with 1 floor, 4 lenses and 6 enemies. Pass bar: 70%+ of playtesters start a second run without being asked, plus a strong reaction to the slice from a press or influencer preview.

### 8.6 Tiny Orchard: premium cozy sim {: .newpage }

**Pitch:** *Grow a small orchard, graft trees to invent new fruit and sell your harvest at the weekly village market. No timers, no ads, just seasons.*

| Snapshot | |
|---|---|
| **Genre** | Cozy farming / breeding sim |
| **Audience** | 20–45 cozy-game fans (Stardew Valley is in the paid top 10); skews female; plays to relax |
| **Session** | One in-game day = 5–10 minutes; players choose when the day ends |
| **Price** | $4.99 up-front + a $2.99 story expansion; no ads |
| **Platforms** | iOS + Android; Switch/PC later |
| **Our twist** | *Grafting:* combine two trees to discover hybrid fruit. More than 100 varieties to collect, each with a name, flavour and look. |

**How it plays**

- Plant saplings, water and prune them, and protect them from frost and pests.
- **Graft** a branch from one tree onto another. The next season, a new hybrid fruit appears and its traits come from both parents (colour, size, sweetness, season).
- Harvest and sell at the weekly market. Villagers post requests, e.g. *"something tart and red for my pie"*.
- Seasons shape what grows. Winter is for planning, grafting and decorating.

**Game loops**

<div class="loop core"><div class="loop-label">Core loop · one in-game day (5–10 min)</div><div class="steps"><span>Tend trees (water, prune, protect)</span><b>→</b><span>Graft / plant</span><b>→</b><span>Harvest ripe fruit</span><b>→</b><span>Fill villager requests</span><b>→</b><span>End the day <em>↺</em></span></div></div>
<div class="loop meta"><div class="loop-label">Meta loop · weekly (in game)</div><div class="steps"><span>Market day: sell + earn</span><b>→</b><span>Buy saplings, tools, plots</span><b>→</b><span>Discover new hybrids (fruit book)</span><b>→</b><span>Villager friendships deepen <em>↺</em></span></div></div>
<div class="loop long"><div class="loop-label">Long-term loop · seasons and years</div><div class="steps"><span>Seasons change what grows</span><b>→</b><span>Annual orchard fair competition</span><b>→</b><span>Expand and decorate the orchard</span><b>→</b><span>Story expansion <em>↺</em></span></div></div>

**Business model:** $4.99 up-front with the full game included. A $2.99 story expansion launches 4–6 months later, followed by free seasonal updates. A strong candidate for Apple Arcade and Play Pass.

**Art & audio:** hand-painted 2D in a storybook style, with soft ambient weather and an acoustic soundtrack. Every hybrid fruit gets a unique illustration.

**Risks & mitigation**

- *Many cozy games compete for this audience.* Keep the scope narrow and make grafting the clear differentiator, with no attempt to become a full farming RPG.
- *Content production (100+ fruits).* Build fruit from modular parts (shape × colour × pattern) so variety is affordable.

**Prototype test (4–6 weeks):** one season with 15 hybrids. Pass bar: playtesters keep grafting to "see what happens" and average 3+ voluntary days per session; store-page wishlists or pre-registrations are also tracked.

## 9. Recommended next steps {: .newpage }

1. **Pick the division's strategy:** a hybrid-casual puzzle portfolio as the primary track and one premium game as the secondary track (recommended), or focus on a single track. Then start the three Wave 1 prototypes from section 8 (Dockside Dispatch, Knot Loose, Paw Logic).
2. **Choose the tech stack:** Unity or Godot for cross-platform builds, plus an ad mediation SDK (AppLovin MAX or ironSource LevelPlay), analytics (Firebase or GameAnalytics) and an attribution provider.
3. **Set up the test pipeline:** a store-page "fake door" or a playable ad test, followed by soft launch in lower-cost markets (e.g. Philippines, Canada, Australia), and only then a global launch.
4. **Run a deeper analysis of the chosen genre:** retention and monetisation benchmarks, a breakdown of the top 20 games in that genre, and their ad creatives.
5. **Plan legal and store compliance:** privacy (ATT and GDPR), loot-box disclosure rules, age ratings and payment policy differences by region.
